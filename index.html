My Journey

From Circuit Boards to Neural Networks

My academic journey began in Power Electronics at Isfahan University of Technology, where I developed a fascination with circuit design and energy systems. I spent countless hours in labs designing DC-DC converters and optimizing power systems.

In 2024, I found myself increasingly drawn to artificial intelligence. What started as curiosity turned into a research direction when I discovered Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL). During my MS at the University of New Haven, I worked on the security and trustworthiness of multi-agent systems - building frameworks to characterize, detect, and mitigate deceptive behavior, from adversarial communication to strategic manipulation.

I am now a PhD student in Electrical & Computer Engineering at the University of Florida, advised by Dr. Jie Fu. My work has moved from the security of small teams to the behavior of large populations: when hundreds of agents act together, how do you make guarantees about the group rather than about any one member of it?

The engineering instinct I picked up designing converters turns out to matter here. A control engineer wants a bound they can point to before the system is deployed, not a plot showing it usually works. That is the standard I'm trying to bring to learned multi-agent policies.

Research Interests

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

How multiple agents learn to coordinate, cooperate, and communicate effectively in complex environments.

Mean-Field & Population-Scale Control

Describing a swarm by its distribution rather than agent by agent, so that coordination stays tractable as the population grows into the hundreds.

Safe & Constrained Reinforcement Learning

Enforcing safety requirements that must hold with high probability, not merely on average; and certifying them for the finite systems we actually deploy.

Robot Learning & Autonomous Navigation

Applying RL and MARL to real robots for robust control, navigation, and safe autonomy under sensing and actuation noise.

Current Research

Safety and Coordination at Population Scale

At the University of Florida, I work with Dr. Jie Fu on multi-agent reinforcement learning for large populations of agents. The motivating question: when a swarm must reach a goal while staying clear of unsafe regions, the specification applies to the distribution of agents rather than to any individual one; and satisfying such a specification in expectation is not the same as satisfying it reliably.

My interests here span mean-field methods that keep the problem tractable at scale, constraint formulations that survive an unknown environment model, and the gap between asymptotic guarantees and the finite systems that actually get deployed.

Previously: Deception and Deception Mitigation in Multi-Agent Systems

During my MS at the SAIL Lab (University of New Haven), I worked with Dr. Vahid Behzadan on ensuring multi-agent systems remain robust against deceptive manipulation.

Our method, Communicative Power Regularization (CPR), enables cooperative agents to maintain effective coordination even under adversarial or misaligned communication. This work was published at ECAI 2025 and selected for poster presentation at CoCoMARL 2025 and NYRL 2025. I also presented it at Hartford AI Day 2025.
